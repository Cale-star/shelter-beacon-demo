The first field test broke an assumption.

I assumed a report that was sent had arrived. 784 packets later, the data said otherwise, and the design changed twice because of it.

Range test 1: walk away until it stops.

October 18, 2026. Two Heltec V3 boards at full power. One stayed put and logged to a laptop; the other was carried away, sending a numbered packet every 2.2 seconds with no repeats. Each dot is one packet that arrived.

Signal strength of every packet received

dBm, over the 41 minutes of the test. Lower is weaker.

The same test as numbers

What it showed.

  • 7.5%

    of packets vanished once the signal got down to −100 dBm or weaker: 49 of 651. They went missing in 26 dropouts, most of them one packet, the longest ten in a row. No warning. One lost packet could have been the one that said "medical."Outside the two long gaps

    with nothing at all. Behind terrain the link didn't fade, it stopped, and 186 packets in a row were lost.The red band in the chart

    of silence later in the walk, but this one wasn't the radio. The carried board restarted: its packet count went back to zero. Why it restarted wasn't logged.The grey band in the chart

  • −122 dBm

    was the weakest packet still decoded, about 7 dB above the calculated limit of −129 dBm. The radio performs close to what the maths predicts.Sensitivity = −174 + 10·log₁₀(125 kHz) + 6 dB noise figure − 12.5 dB

What changed because of it.

Every report is now sent three times. Eighteen of the 26 dropouts were a single packet, and a second copy half a second later should get past those. But losses came in bunches, so the copies don't fail independently, and I won't put a percentage on it until a test counts them.

Then headquarters started answering. Copies can't cross a 22-second dropout, let alone a seven-minute blackout. So the box now waits for a confirmation, and if none comes it retries after 5, 15 and 30 seconds, then every minute, until it gets through.

The box only talks when something changes. A packet every two seconds wasted battery and airtime to say nothing new.

What this test can't tell you. It didn't record distance, so there's no range figure here and I won't quote one. How often three copies get through is still unmeasured. The confirm-and-retry firmware compiles and passes its simulated tests, and still needs time on real radios.

Range test 2 is next, and it has GPS.

The box will send a full three-copy report every five seconds while a phone riding with it records a GPS track. Afterwards a script joins every packet to a position.

That gives three things this page is missing: how far it reaches, how delivery falls off with distance, and how often three copies get through when single packets don't.
