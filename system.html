A report is 31 bytes, built to survive a bad link.

The box doesn't stream data. It says one short thing when something changes, says it up to three times, and waits to hear that headquarters got it.

Build a report yourself.

Set the switches and the people count. This is the exact text the box transmits. Pick any piece to see what it means.

Every report carries the whole state, not just what changed. If one is lost, the next one repairs the picture.

What headquarters makes of it

Sent is not the same as received.

Radio links drop packets. So the box keeps going until headquarters answers, and it tells the person at the box which of the two has happened.

    The ranking is a score you can check by hand.

    When several shelters report at once, someone has to decide who gets the first truck. The dashboard adds up a small score per shelter and explains it on screen, so nobody has to trust a black box.

    A score of 3 or more is high priority and rings an alarm at headquarters until a person acknowledges it. A score of 2 is medium.
